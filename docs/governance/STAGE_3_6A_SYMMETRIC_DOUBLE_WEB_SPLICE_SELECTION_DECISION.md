# FRP Master Connection — Stage 3.6A Symmetric Double-Web-Splice Plates — Selection Decision

## Decision

The next major shear-connection family begins as:

**Stage 3.6A — FRP W/I Beam-to-Beam Symmetric Double Web Splice Plates**

Normal product label:

**Beam connection — Symmetric double web splice plates**

This product remains under **Shear Connections**.

Stage 3.6A is deliberately limited to a common, concentric first slice:

- two collinear, identical FRP W/I beams;
- one positive and one negative FRP flat splice plate, one on each side of the beam webs;
- two independent physical web-bolt groups, one on each side of the beam-end joint;
- one positive beam-end gap;
- signed axial force, major shear, and minor shear;
- zero user-applied moment.

No flange splice is included.

No single-sided web splice plate is included in RC1.

## Source interpretation

ASCE/SEI 74-23 Section 8.1 explicitly recognizes splice plates as connection elements and covers lap-shear configurations with loading principally in the plane of the connected members.

Section 8.2.1 identifies braced-system and column-splice connections within Chapter 8 design requirements.

Section 8.3.4.2 requires splice connections to transmit the applicable forces and to account for eccentricity of bolt-group centroids. For flexural-member parts subjected to shear, the shear at the splice and eccentricity effects are part of the required transfer.

Stage 3.6A therefore retains the exact moments generated by offset bolt-group centroids.

However, RC1 does **not** authorize user-applied member bending moment at the splice. It is a shear-category slice for cases where the required user-applied flexural moment at the splice is zero under structural analysis.

Required limitation:

`WEB_SPLICE_MEMBER_FLEXURAL_MOMENT_TRANSFER = NOT_AUTHORIZED_IN_RC1`.

## Splice frame

Define a right-handed splice frame:

- `L_S`: beam longitudinal axis, positive from Beam A / left to Beam B / right;
- `V_S`: vertical direction in the beam-web plane;
- `T_S`: beam-web through-thickness normal.

Require:

`L_S × V_S = T_S`.

Joint plane:

`L_S = 0`.

Beam A is on the negative-L side.

Beam B is on the positive-L side.

## Beam geometry

Both beams are W/I profiles with identical dimensions/material basis in RC1.

No unequal beam profiles or offsets.

No inclination or relative roll.

The two web centroidal planes are collinear.

## Beam-end gap

The two beam ends do not bear directly in RC1.

A positive user-controlled beam-end gap is required.

Controlled default:

`0.5 in`.

Beam A end plane:

`L_S = -gap/2`.

Beam B end plane:

`L_S = +gap/2`.

Because the gap is positive, axial compression in RC1 is intentionally transferred through the splice-plate system rather than direct beam-end bearing.

No end-bearing partition method is required.

## Splice plates

Exactly two identical FRP flat splice plates:

- positive plate on `+T_S` side;
- negative plate on `-T_S` side.

They are exact mirrors and are locked identical in RC1.

Each plate is continuous across the beam-end gap and both bolt groups.

Default each plate:

- length along `L_S`: `16 in`;
- height along `V_S`: `8 in`;
- thickness along `T_S`: `0.5 in`.

The plate pultrusion/material `LW` direction is along `L_S`.

The plate `CW` direction is along `V_S`.

The plate `TT` direction is along `T_S`.

## Physical bolt groups

There are two independent physical groups:

- `BEAM_A_WEB_SPLICE_GROUP`;
- `BEAM_B_WEB_SPLICE_GROUP`.

Each bolt passes:

`Positive Splice Plate -> Beam Web -> Negative Splice Plate`.

Each group is a three-layer common physical-bolt group.

No duplicate bolt identity.

No internal hardware.

Default each group:

- 2 rows;
- 2 bolts per row;
- group centroid `4 in` from the joint plane;
- longitudinal group width/gauge `3 in`;
- vertical pitch/gauge `3 in` under the shared fixed-grid convention;
- nominal bolt diameter `0.5 in`;
- hole diameter `0.563 in`.

The two groups are exact mirrors about the joint plane.

## Transfer-action convention

The normal user inputs are:

- **Axial force** `P_L`, signed along the beam longitudinal transfer axis;
- **Major shear** `V_V`, signed along the web vertical axis;
- **Minor shear** `V_T`, signed along the web-normal / bolt-axis direction.

User-applied moments are zero.

`P_L > 0` means axial tension across the splice.

`P_L < 0` means axial compression across the splice.

The canonical transfer force is:

`F_tr = (P_L, V_V, V_T)`.

The force exerted at the Beam A interface and Beam B interface is equal and opposite under the common splice-frame convention.

## Eccentricity from group centroids

The canonical transfer reference is the joint plane at the web centroid:

`r_J = (0,0,0)`.

Default group centroids:

- Beam A group: `r_A = (-4,0,0) in`;
- Beam B group: `r_B = (+4,0,0) in`.

The backend translates the canonical transfer action exactly to each group.

For Beam A:

`F_A = F_tr`

`M_A = (r_J-r_A) × F_A`.

For Beam B:

`F_B = -F_tr`

`M_B = (r_J-r_B) × F_B`.

Generated group moments are mandatory transfer/equilibrium moments, not user-applied connection moments.

## Default transfer benchmark

Default:

- Axial force `0 kip`;
- Major shear `-10 kip`;
- Minor shear `0 kip`.

Expected:

Beam A group:

- `F_A = (0,-10,0) kip`;
- `M_A = (0,0,-40) kip-in`.

Beam B group:

- `F_B = (0,+10,0) kip`;
- `M_B = (0,0,-40) kip-in`.

Each group, when shifted back to the joint reference with its own interface force, recovers zero user-applied joint moment.

The plate-pair free-body actions are equal/opposite and equilibrated.

## Component-design demand philosophy

At each beam-side interface:

### Beam web

The beam web layer receives 100% of that physical group demand.

### Splice-plate pair

The splice-plate pair system receives 100% of that physical group demand.

After exact plate-pair symmetry proof:

- positive splice plate layer = 50% of per-bolt in-plane demand;
- negative splice plate layer = 50%.

The plate pair is one transfer system, not two independent full-demand plate systems.

## Material-direction behavior

Both the beam web and splice plates use region-specific material bases.

With default alignment:

### Axial force

Parallel to `LW` in:

- Beam A web;
- Beam B web;
- positive splice plate;
- negative splice plate.

### Major shear

Parallel to `CW` in those same web/plate regions.

### Minor shear

Parallel to `TT` / bolt axes and is outside the supported in-plane local-response path.

## Local checks

Existing accepted Stage 2.5A / 2.5B / 2.6 mechanics may be reused for local beam-web and splice-plate bolt-hole checks where their current applicability holds.

No new demand/resistance equation.

## Deliberate limitations

Stage 3.6A does not authorize:

- user-applied beam bending moment;
- flange splice;
- single-sided splice plate;
- unequal beams;
- splice-plate inter-group body transfer capacity;
- common-bolt metallic double-shear capacity;
- minor-shear bolt-axis tension/prying response;
- global beam member design;
- slip-critical behavior.

Always retain as applicable:

- `WEB_SPLICE_MEMBER_FLEXURAL_MOMENT_TRANSFER = NOT_AUTHORIZED_IN_RC1`;
- `WEB_SPLICE_PLATE_INTERGROUP_BODY_TRANSFER = NOT_EVALUATED`;
- `WEB_SPLICE_COMMON_BOLT_DOUBLE_SHEAR = NOT_EVALUATED`;
- `WEB_SPLICE_MINOR_SHEAR_BOLT_AXIS_RESPONSE = NOT_EVALUATED`.

Ordinary whole-splice `PASS` is prohibited while required checks are not evaluated.

Supported local numerical failure retains `FAIL` precedence.

## Frozen-family boundary

Stage 2.3, Stage 3.2, Stage 3.3, Stage 3.4, and Stage 3.5 freeze tags remain immutable.

No Stage 3.5 behavior is modified.
